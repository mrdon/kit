## What makes a good question

- **Fun and surprising:** a regular person hears the answer and says "no way". Cut dry stats, specs and corporate figures unless they're genuinely striking.
- **Mostly not guessable.** One easy one is fine; mark it.
- **Clear and simple:** one or two short sentences, plain words, reads cleanly aloud.
- **Explain anything obscure** in a few words: places, customs, non-English words, niche events ("Ireland's 5,000-year-old Newgrange tomb").
- **Context in the question:** if the scale is unfamiliar, give an anchor ("NASA requires at least 60.8 psi").
- **Answers are short whole numbers:**
  - at least 5
  - no decimals
  - no more than 4 digits where possible, 5 at most; for big figures, ask "in thousands" or "in millions", and give the figure in that unit with the remainder dropped
  - year answers are fine but don't lean on them
- **Never a threshold answer** ("more than how many"). State the unit.
- **Dated questions are great:** "In 2004, how many..." is hard to look up on a phone. Records broken often always get a year; never "the current record".
- **True today:** check the latest news on anything that changes.
- **No downers:** no disasters, deaths, injuries, failures, war or anything grim.

## Sourcing rules

- Every number must come from a page you fetched and read in this session. Use WebSearch to find, then WebFetch to confirm. Nothing from memory, nothing from a search snippet alone.
- Give the exact URL and a short verbatim quote containing the number.
- Prefer official and primary sources: the organization itself, Guinness, government, major news. Wikipedia and fan or entertainment sites only when nothing better turns up; say so.
- Cut a figure when sources disagree or when it is itself a loose estimate.
- If the page supports only part of a claim, reword the question to what it literally says, or drop it.
